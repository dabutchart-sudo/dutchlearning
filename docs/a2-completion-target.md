# Zin A2 completion target

## Purpose

Zin A2 is the next finite stage after A1. It is for slightly longer everyday situations: reporting what happened, giving a reason, comparing, saying what you are going to do, and giving a short opinion, then using those together.

This is a product curriculum target, not a claim that completing Zin is an official CEFR qualification. Course keeps the heading **A2 · Beyond the basics**. A2.1 is a real topic on the path. It stays locked until the A1 checkpoint has been passed, and it does not count as retained. A2.2–A2.8 are not in the app. This target does not build the A1 checkpoint.

## Unlock

A2.1 stays locked until the A1 checkpoint (A1.26) has been passed. Later A2 topics stay locked until the previous A2 topic has passed its normal mastery proof. A2.8 stays closed until A2.1–A2.7 exist and each has had its normal delayed retention check.

The daily 20, the five-new-card ceiling, written mastery, and written retention stay as they are. A2 does not add a second normal session.

## Quality bar

Each A2 topic has to meet the standard already used by the live A1 course, including two checks that some A1 topics received only after release.

- The lesson states the rule, one worked sentence, two pattern notes, and two examples taken from that topic’s practice pool. Proof sentences are never used as lesson examples.
- The reasoning method is taught before independent Dutch is required, then the help fades. The learner should be able to solve the question from what Zin has taught.
- At least 40 recorded practice answers are required before a test, and the topic has at least 20 distinct practice contexts.
- At least 90 distinct unseen proof sentences are available, enough for repeated failed mastery days, one pass, and later retention. A word used in proof has already appeared in practice. The proof audit reports no gaps.
- Mastery stays a written 20-question check: 19/20 overall, and at least 9/10 in each direction. Retention stays a written 10/10 check on a later day.
- Course states, in plain language, what the learner can now do.
- A native speaker has accepted the new Dutch, and the lesson has been checked on the installed phone, before the topic is called live.

Listening and speaking stay optional practice on sentences the topic has already taught. They do not replace the written tests.

## Course map

Eight topics, then stop. New A2 material should improve one of these topics rather than add A2.9 onwards. Language stays everyday and high-frequency. Each topic reuses A1 words wherever the new pattern can be seen clearly.

| Concept | Status | Learner capability | Main new language |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A2.1 | Published in V5.1.188, locked until A1.26 | Report a simple past event | Perfect already known from A1, plus a small set of imperfect forms where Dutch needs them |
| A2.2 | Planned | Give a reason | One `omdat` clause, verb at the end; `daarom` when the reason comes second |
| A2.3 | Planned | Compare and prefer | `meer` / `minder`, common `-er` forms, `het …st`, `liever` |
| A2.4 | Planned | Say what you are going to do | `gaan` plus an infinitive, beyond the existing S3 appointment chunks |
| A2.5 | Planned | Give a short opinion | `ik vind`, `te` plus an adjective, one familiar way to agree or disagree |
| A2.6 | Planned | Explain a past problem | The live S1 situation, plus what went wrong, why, and what help was needed |
| A2.7 | Planned | Tell a short connected story | A familiar day or trip using A2.1–A2.6, with no new grammar |
| A2.8 | Planned, closed | A2 integrated checkpoint | Unseen mixed situations across the A2 topics |

`'t kofschip` is taught in A2.1 only when the learner has to choose `-te/-ten` or `-de/-den`. It is a temporary aid for that spelling choice, not a topic of its own.

## Completion evidence

A learner reaches Zin’s A2 completion state only when:

1. A1.26 has been passed;
2. every required A2 topic has passed its normal mastery proof and retention proof;
3. the A2.8 checkpoint is passed using unseen material and without answer-revealing assistance;
4. the checkpoint samples both understanding and Dutch production;
5. the checkpoint includes more than one situation, so memorising one topic cannot satisfy it;
6. normal daily-session limits remain intact.

Passing A2.8 is Zin’s record of retained A2. It is not a certificate.

## Development rules

- Prefer useful sentences and everyday situations over grammatical completeness.
- Reuse known vocabulary so the new structure is visible.
- Introduce genuinely new vocabulary in controlled amounts.
- Teach the useful reasoning step before independent production, then fade it.
- Do not add broad relative clauses, a wide past-tense system, or low-frequency grammar in order to make the list look complete.
- Keep the existing lesson, practice, mastery proof, and retention proof progression.
- Do not weaken proof requirements to make the content fit.
- Build A2.8 last.
