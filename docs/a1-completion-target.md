# Zin A1 completion target

## Purpose

Zin should stop expanding A1 by simply adding another grammar topic. This document defines the finite learner capability that the A1 course is working toward.

A1 is complete when the learner can understand and produce short, useful Dutch for familiar everyday situations with the existing adaptive support system, and can retain the required structures after the normal mastery and retention checks.

This is a product curriculum target, not a claim that completing Zin is itself an official CEFR qualification.

## What the current course already covers well

The current A1.1–A1.16 sequence gives substantial practice in:

- basic present-tense sentence construction and common verb forms;
- personal subjects and common everyday vocabulary;
- `niet` and the later `niet`/`geen` distinction;
- question words and yes/no questions;
- perfect tense with `zijn` and `hebben`;
- separable verbs;
- modal verbs plus infinitive;
- inversion when time/place information comes first;
- sentence recognition, construction, typed production, correction and listening-style exercises.

Some later concepts deliberately consolidate earlier material. They should not be removed merely because their grammar label overlaps: the later concept can require a broader contrast or more independent production.

## Remaining A1 capability gaps

The remaining course should be organised around these capabilities rather than isolated grammar labels.

### 1. People and things: articles, plurals and reference

Learner can use common nouns with `de`, `het` and `een`, recognise/use common plural forms, and refer back to familiar people/things with basic pronouns. Exact article choice for every Dutch noun is not required before A1 completion; high-frequency vocabulary is the target.

### 2. Possession and description

Learner can say whose something is and give simple descriptions: possessives such as `mijn`, `jouw`, `zijn`, `haar`, `ons/onze`; common adjective patterns; simple colours, size and condition.

### 3. Quantity, numbers, dates and time

Learner can understand and produce useful numbers, prices, ages, clock times, days/dates and simple quantities (`veel`, `weinig`, `een beetje`, common counting expressions).

### 4. Place, movement and directions

Learner can say where something is, where they are going/coming from, and understand/give very simple directions using high-frequency prepositions and location expressions.

### 5. Everyday requests and transactions

Learner can make simple polite requests, order/buy something, ask the price, ask for help or repetition, and respond to common service interactions. This should reuse questions, modals, negation and numbers rather than teach them again in isolation.

### 6. Daily life and personal information

Learner can exchange short information about home, family, work, routine, food, transport, appointments and leisure. This is primarily a functional consolidation strand using grammar already learned.

### 7. Connecting short ideas

Learner can join simple clauses or ideas with high-frequency connectors such as `en`, `maar`, `of`, `want` and simple sequencing words. A1 does not require broad subordinate-clause mastery.

### 8. Integrated A1 communication

Learner can handle short mixed situations without being told which grammar rule is being tested: introduce themselves, arrange a simple plan, describe a routine, make a purchase/request, ask and answer basic questions, and report a simple recent event.

## Explicit learning-technique layer

Zin must teach the reasoning tools needed to solve Dutch questions, not rely on the learner having acquired those tools from another app. A learner should not be able to answer Zin material only because an external course previously explained the underlying pattern.

For each curriculum concept, development should identify any useful rule, mnemonic, decomposition method or visual framework that helps the learner reason out an answer. Teach that technique at the point where it first solves a real problem, then practise using it explicitly before gradually fading the scaffolding.

The default progression is:

1. **Teach** — briefly explain the useful rule or technique in context.
2. **Guided application** — expose the reasoning steps and let the learner apply them.
3. **Faded support** — remove individual prompts as the technique becomes familiar.
4. **Independent use** — require the correct Dutch without exposing the reasoning path.
5. **Retention** — revisit both the language and, when needed, the technique after spacing.

Candidate techniques include:

- verb-stem construction and spelling changes;
- inversion cues and visual sentence-order frameworks;
- separable-verb decomposition and reconstruction;
- reliable `de`/`het` categories while treating lexical exceptions honestly;
- productive plural and diminutive patterns;
- mnemonic/rule systems such as `'t kofschip` / “soft ketchup” for `-te/-ten` versus `-de/-den` when past-tense morphology is actually introduced.

Do not introduce a mnemonic before the learner has encountered the problem it solves, and do not turn mnemonic recall into the learning objective. The objective is independent Dutch production; the technique is temporary cognitive scaffolding.

As A1.17–A1.26 are developed or refined, explicitly audit both new and already-taught prerequisite concepts for missing explanation. Where Zin currently tests a concept without having taught the learner a usable way to reason about it, add the teaching/scaffolding before increasing difficulty. This is a course-quality requirement, not an optional enrichment feature.

## Completion evidence

A learner reaches Zin's A1 completion state only when:

1. every required A1 concept has passed its normal mastery proof and retention proof;
2. the integrated A1.26 checkpoint is passed using unseen material and without answer-revealing assistance;
3. the checkpoint samples both understanding and Dutch production rather than recognition alone;
4. the checkpoint includes multiple functional domains, so memorising one topic cannot satisfy it;
5. normal daily-session limits remain intact — completion is earned across study days, not by an oversized final session.

## Development rules from this point

- Prefer useful sentences and everyday situations over grammatical completeness.
- Reuse known vocabulary where possible so a new structure is visible in context.
- Introduce genuinely new vocabulary in controlled amounts.
- Keep English→Dutch production gradual but unavoidable by the end of A1.
- Teach useful reasoning techniques before expecting the learner to apply the corresponding Dutch concept independently.
- Fade technique scaffolding as mastery grows; do not make permanent hints a substitute for learning.
- Do not turn A1 into A2 by adding broad subordinate clauses, complex relative clauses, extensive past-tense distinctions or low-frequency grammar.
- Preserve the existing lesson → practice → mastery proof → retention proof progression.
- Each new concept needs enough unique practice/proof material for the existing proof engine; do not weaken proof requirements to make content fit.
- A1.26 should be built last, after the preceding capability gaps are represented in real content.

## Immediate next bounded batch

Continue the capability-driven A1 expansion while applying the explicit learning-technique audit to each concept. Before adding harder production for a concept, verify that Zin itself has taught the learner the rule, pattern or reasoning method needed to solve it; add guided and fading scaffolding where that explanation is currently missing.
